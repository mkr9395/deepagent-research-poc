# Apple vs. Tesla: revenue, profitability and risk

## Executive Summary
- Apple reported **$478.834 billion** in revenue for FY2026, about **5.0 times** Tesla’s approximately **$94.83 billion** in FY2025; the fiscal periods differ. [1][3]
- Revenue rose approximately **15.1%** at Apple and fell approximately **2.9%** at Tesla, a roughly **18.0-percentage-point** difference in calculated growth. [1][3]
- Apple’s calculated net margin was **27.6%**, versus Tesla’s **4.1%**; Apple’s net-income figure comes from a secondary reproduction of its filing. [1][2][3]
- The retrieved risk disclosures highlight Apple’s supply-chain and IT continuity exposure versus Tesla’s dependence on EV demand and autonomous-driving adoption. They do not support a comprehensive ranking of overall risk. [3][4]

## Revenue and profitability

| Metric | Apple FY2026, ended Sept. 26, 2026 | Tesla FY2025, ended Dec. 31, 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $478.834bn [1] | approximately $94.83bn [3] |
| Revenue growth, year over year | 15.1%, calculated [1] | approximately −2.9%, calculated [3] |
| Consolidated net income | $132.175bn [2] | $3.855bn [3] |
| Net margin | 27.6%, calculated [1][2] | approximately 4.1%, calculated [3] |
| Operating margin | 33.0%, calculated [1] | Not verified |

**Key differences.** Apple’s net margin was approximately **23.5 percentage points higher**, calculated from the displayed rounded margins; its revenue was roughly **$384 billion greater**, calculated from the reported figures. [1][2][3] This indicates both greater scale and substantially more net profit per revenue dollar in these periods. Net margins use consolidated net income divided by revenue. The years ended nearly nine months apart, so the figures are not strictly contemporaneous. Tesla’s revenue and growth figures are rounded; Apple’s net income was retrieved from a secondary reproduction rather than the primary filing excerpt.

## Risk analysis

- **Apple:** Its filing identifies disruption to the complex IT systems used by Apple and its global supply chain, including from power or telecommunications failures, war and cyberattacks. It also identifies trade-receivables credit risk; the retrieved evidence does not quantify that exposure. [4]
- **Tesla:** Its filing states that growth depends on EV demand and adoption of autonomous-driving solutions. It also warns that initiatives tied to its 2025 CEO Performance Award could be misaligned with consumer demand and divert investment from other opportunities. [3]
- **Key differences:** The cited Apple risks center on operational continuity and credit, while Tesla’s cited disclosures more directly emphasize product demand, technology adoption and capital allocation. This is a comparison of disclosed examples, not proof that either company faces more total risk. [3][4]

## Overall Comparison
Apple leads on reported scale, revenue growth and net profitability in the different fiscal periods reviewed. The disclosed risk examples suggest different operating sensitivities, but the available evidence cannot establish which company is riskier overall.

**Evidence gaps and uncertainty:** Tesla’s operating margin was not verified, so operating profitability cannot be compared. The retrieved risk evidence is not comprehensive for financial, regulatory, legal or competitive risks for either company. The enforcement search timed out; no conclusion about recent enforcement for either company should be inferred. Apple’s net income is from a secondary source, and the fiscal years differ.
