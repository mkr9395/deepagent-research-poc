# NVIDIA vs. Tesla: Fiscal 2025 Revenue and Risk

## Executive Summary
- NVIDIA reported $130.5 billion in revenue, up 114%; Tesla reported $94.827 billion, down 2.93% (calculated). Their fiscal years ended more than 11 months apart, so this is not a same-period comparison. [1][2]
- NVIDIA’s growth was driven by Data Center demand; Tesla’s automotive revenue fell, partly offset by growth in energy and services. [1][2]
- Available evidence highlights NVIDIA’s direct-customer concentration and Tesla’s factory, battery-supply and international-operation risks. It does **not** support a complete or like-for-like ranking of their risks. [3][4]

## Revenue

| Fiscal 2025 | NVIDIA | Tesla |
|---|---:|---:|
| Year-end | January 26, 2025 | December 31, 2025 |
| Revenue | $130.497bn [1][5] | $94.827bn [2] |
| Year-over-year change | +114% [1] | −2.93%, calculated [2] |

**NVIDIA:** The company attributed Data Center growth to demand for Hopper accelerated computing, including for generative AI. Data Center revenue rose 142%. [1]

**Tesla:** Automotive revenue fell from $77.070 billion to $69.526 billion; energy generation and storage increased from $10.086 billion to $12.771 billion, and services and other from $10.534 billion to $12.530 billion. Tesla attributed energy growth primarily to higher Megapack and Powerwall deployments, partly offset by lower Megapack average selling prices. [2]

**Key differences:** NVIDIA’s reported revenue was $35.670 billion higher (calculated), and its growth was approximately 116.93 percentage points faster (calculated). This contrasts rapid Data Center expansion with an automotive decline partly offset by Tesla’s other revenue lines. NVIDIA’s exact revenue figure is from a secondary financial-statement compilation; its own annual report confirms the rounded $130.5 billion figure and growth rate. The periods differ substantially. [1][2][5]

## Risk

**NVIDIA:** Three direct customers accounted for 12%, 11% and 11% of fiscal 2025 sales, respectively, primarily attributable to Compute & Networking. Together that is 34% (calculated), indicating direct-customer concentration; direct customers are not necessarily the ultimate end customers. [3]

**Tesla:** Its fiscal 2025 fourth-quarter update cautioned about factory ramps, procuring battery cells—including through its own manufacturing—and international operations and expansion. These are company-identified risks in the update, not verified quotations from the December 2025 10-K risk factors. [4]

**Key differences:** The available evidence identifies concentration in NVIDIA’s direct customers versus Tesla’s operational and supply exposures. These disclosures are different kinds of evidence, not comparable measures of risk magnitude; neither establishes which company is more exposed overall. [3][4]

## Overall Comparison
NVIDIA had higher and much faster-growing reported fiscal 2025 revenue, while Tesla’s revenue declined. The risk evidence points to different exposures but is insufficient to rank them.

**Evidence gaps and uncertainty:** Researchers located both fiscal 2025 annual filings but could not retrieve substantive Item 1A risk-factor text for either company. Consequently, a complete comparison of their principal disclosed risks is unverified. The fiscal year-ends and exposure windows also differ; NVIDIA’s exact revenue amount is secondary-sourced. [1][2][5][6]
