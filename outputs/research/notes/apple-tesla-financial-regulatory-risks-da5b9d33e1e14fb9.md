## Research Question
What are Apple’s and Tesla’s major financial and regulatory risks, including recent regulatory actions and company-specific exposures?

## Key Findings
- [Fact] Apple’s FY2026 filing identifies potential inventory and other asset write-downs and purchase-commitment cancellation risk. These are company-disclosed risks, not reported losses. [Apple 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320193/000032019326000026/aapl-20260926.htm)
- [Interpretation] Tesla’s debt and minimum lease commitments create future payment obligations; the figures alone do not establish a liquidity shortfall. [Tesla 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000162828026003952/tsla-20251231.htm)
- [Fact] On March 19, 2026, Reuters reported that NHTSA had escalated an investigation covering approximately 3.2 million Tesla vehicles with Full Self-Driving, citing concerns about detection or driver warnings in poor visibility. This is an investigation, not a finding of a defect or an ordered recall. [Reuters, via U.S. News](https://www.usnews.com/news/top-news/articles/2026-03-19/us-auto-safety-regulator-opens-probe-into-tesla-vehicles-with-fsd)
- [Interpretation] The Tesla investigation creates potential safety-compliance and remediation exposure, but its outcome and cost are not established by the cited report.

## Financial Facts
- Apple: FY2026 ended September 26, 2026; a monetary value for the inventory and purchase-commitment risks was not found in the available results. [Company 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/320193/000032019326000026/aapl-20260926.htm)
- Tesla: At December 31, 2025, debt principal was $8.18 billion, including $1.58 billion current; minimum lease payments were $7.96 billion, including $1.32 billion due within 12 months. These are distinct obligations. [Company FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1318605/000162828026003952/tsla-20251231.htm)

## Caveats

The filing extracts retrieved were limited, so this is not a comprehensive inventory of either company’s risks. Tesla’s investigation is sourced to Reuters rather than the underlying regulator document, and its later status was not verified.
