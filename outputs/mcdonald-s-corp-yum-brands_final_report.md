# McDonald’s vs. Yum! Brands: Revenue Growth, Profitability, Risks and Outlook

## Executive Summary

- **Revenue growth:** Yum! Brands’ revenue grew a calculated **8.8%** in FY2025, compared with **3.7%** for McDonald’s—a lead of approximately **5.1 percentage points**. McDonald’s was larger, with $26.9 billion of revenue versus Yum’s $8.2 billion. Both fiscal years ended December 31, 2025. [1, 2]
- **Profitability:** McDonald’s generated a calculated **46.1% operating margin** and **31.9% net margin**, compared with Yum’s **31.3%** and **19.0%**. Differences in franchise and company-operated revenue mix limit the meaning of a direct margin comparison. [1, 2]
- **Risks:** Both disclose cyber threats. McDonald’s explicitly highlights risks originating with franchisees and other third parties; Yum recorded $27 million of net bad-debt expense in 2025 and previously reported adverse effects from geopolitical conflicts. The evidence does not support a comprehensive ranking of the companies’ overall risk exposure. [3–5]
- **Outlook:** McDonald’s guidance points to approximately 2,600 gross openings and $3.7–$3.9 billion of capital expenditure in 2026. Yum’s recent evidence highlights Taco Bell and KFC as growth platforms and a major Pizza Hut portfolio change, but comparable current earnings and unit-growth guidance was not found. [6–9]

## Revenue Growth and Profitability

### Side-by-side comparison

All amounts are US dollars, in millions; both companies’ fiscal years ended December 31. Growth and margins are calculated from reported rounded GAAP amounts. Operating margin is operating income divided by revenue; net margin is net income divided by revenue. [1, 2]

| Metric | McDonald’s FY2025 | Yum! FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue, FY2025 | $26,885 | $8,214 |
| Revenue, FY2024 | $25,920 | $7,549 |
| Revenue growth, calculated | 3.7% | 8.8% |
| Operating income/profit | $12,393 | $2,574 |
| Operating margin, calculated | 46.1% | 31.3% |
| Net income | $8,563 | $1,559 |
| Net margin, calculated | 31.9% | 19.0% |

**McDonald’s evidence:** Revenue increased from $25.9 billion to $26.9 billion. Its calculated operating margin rose from **45.2%** in FY2024 to **46.1%** in FY2025, and net margin rose from **31.7%** to **31.9%**. McDonald’s reported revenue growth of 4%, or 2% in constant currency; the difference from the calculated 3.7% reflects rounding. [1]

**Yum evidence:** Revenue increased from $7.5 billion to $8.2 billion. Its calculated operating margin declined from **31.8%** to **31.3%**, and net margin from **19.7%** to **19.0%**. Yum’s FY2025 revenue included $2.945 billion in company sales, $3.473 billion in franchise and property revenues, and $1.796 billion in franchise advertising and other service contributions. [2]

### Key differences

Yum grew revenue approximately **5.1 percentage points faster**, while McDonald’s revenue was about **3.3 times** Yum’s, calculated from the reported figures. McDonald’s operating margin exceeded Yum’s by **14.8 percentage points**, and its net margin by **12.9 points**; McDonald’s margins also improved while Yum’s declined. [1, 2]

**Interpretation and comparability:** These are corporate, not restaurant-level, measures. Franchise fees, company-operated sales and service contributions enter revenue differently; Yum’s detailed mix is available above, but a matching full-year McDonald’s breakdown was not extracted. Operating income also includes special items. Currency affects reported growth, and Yum discussed a prior-year 53rd-week comparison in its adjusted operating-profit results; the reported comparison is not a pure equal-week comparison. Consequently, the margin gap should not be taken as a like-for-like measure of individual restaurant economics. [1, 2, 7]

## Risk Analysis

### McDonald’s

**Evidence:** McDonald’s FY2025 filing identifies potential cyber incidents involving franchisees, vendors, suppliers and service providers. Such incidents could compromise company data or disrupt operations despite mitigation measures. This is a disclosed risk, not evidence of a particular incident. [3]

### Yum! Brands

**Evidence:** Yum’s FY2025 filing identifies ransomware, malicious software, credential stuffing, denial-of-service attacks and other cyber threats. It recorded **$27 million** of net bad-debt expense in FY2025, versus **$28 million** in FY2024; the available excerpt does not establish how much was specifically attributable to franchisees. [4]

In its earlier FY2024 filing, Yum said conflicts in the Middle East and between Russia and Ukraine had adversely affected its business and could continue to do so. This historical disclosure, sourced from a third-party filing reproduction, does **not** establish a specific FY2025 or later impact. [5]

### Key differences

Both companies disclose cyber exposure; the available McDonald’s evidence emphasizes third parties, while Yum’s details several types of attack. Yum’s bad-debt expense provides a quantified financial exposure, but a comparable McDonald’s figure was not found, so a relative credit-risk ranking is not supported. The geopolitical evidence is historical and Yum-specific in the material reviewed; it does not establish that McDonald’s lacks comparable exposure. [3–5]

**Evidence gap:** The retrieved excerpts do not adequately establish either company’s full regulatory/legal, market, competitive and financial risk profile or a ranking of their most material risks. Recent enforcement developments were not verified. The comparison above is therefore partial rather than a conclusion that one company is safer overall.

## Outlook

### McDonald’s

**Management outlook:** An investor overview sets out approximately **2,600 gross global restaurant openings** and **$3.7–$3.9 billion** in capital expenditure for 2026, mostly for new units in the US and International Operated Markets. Approximately 750 openings are expected in those markets, with more than 1,800 by developmental licensees and affiliates. These are plans, not completed openings or spending; gross openings do not account for closures. The overview filename indicates August 2026, but its publication date and whether newer guidance exists were not independently verified. [6]

### Yum! Brands

**Evidence and management context:** In FY2025, Taco Bell same-store sales increased **7%**, and KFC built nearly **3,000 stores**. These are historical results that indicate potential growth platforms, not forecasts of future sales or net unit growth. [7]

Yum announced agreements to sell Pizza Hut for **$2.7 billion** and initially expected approximately **$2.3 billion** in net proceeds, excluding the earn-out. A later company release for Q3 2026 reported an approximately **$1.7 billion pretax gain** related to the sales. The initially expected proceeds are not verified actual cash proceeds, and the reported accounting gain is not the same as cash received. Release dates were not verified in the available note. [8, 9]

### Key differences

McDonald’s has a quantified 2026 expansion and capital-spending plan in the evidence reviewed; Yum’s evidence shows recent brand momentum and a substantial portfolio change rather than comparable current earnings or unit-growth guidance. **Interpretation:** Pizza Hut’s disposition shifts the growth opportunity toward Yum’s remaining brands, making execution and use of proceeds important; the evidence does not support a numerical forecast of which company will grow earnings faster. [6–9]

## Overall Comparison

Yum grew reported revenue faster in the comparable FY2025 periods, while McDonald’s was substantially larger and reported higher and improving corporate margins. Both face cyber exposure; other evidence is too incomplete to rank overall risk. McDonald’s disclosed a quantified 2026 expansion plan, whereas Yum’s outlook evidence centers on existing brand momentum and portfolio transformation. Different revenue mixes, special items and period effects constrain the strength of any simple financial comparison. [1–9]

### Evidence gaps and uncertainty

The risk comparison is incomplete, especially for regulation, competition and McDonald’s financial and market risks. No comparable McDonald’s bad-debt figure, current Yum earnings or unit-growth guidance, or matching McDonald’s revenue-mix breakdown was extracted. The recency of McDonald’s outlook relative to any later guidance, and release dates of the Pizza Hut transaction and Q3 2026 results, were not independently confirmed. Yum’s historical geopolitical disclosure comes from a secondary filing reproduction. Plans, expected proceeds and reported gains should not be treated as realized outcomes or forecasts of returns.
